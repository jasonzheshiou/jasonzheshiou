Research · Findings

The ledger, not the brochure

Eight findings from the A/E benchmark, and five from the underwriting output-variance study — all of them produced by measurements rather than asserted before them. Each states the number, what it changes in practice, and the file it came from, so a reader can argue with the evidence instead of with a summary of it.

2 models × 2 harnesses 23–24 held-out books × 3 runs temperature 1.0 every cell has a denominator

1 · A harness is part of the deployment, not neutral infrastructure

Measured: the same wrapper was worth ≈24 points of precision to Qwen3.8-27B and ≈6 points of accuracy to Qwen3.6-27B. Unaided, 3.8 posted 65.7 % FP/claim — 34.3 % precision — on the same books it reached 58.1 % precision with its harness.

What it changes: what a harness compensates for — how eagerly a model asserts, how much it verifies, whether it reaches for a tool — is behaviour, and behaviour moves when a model is retrained even when its architecture does not. So the artefact under governance is the pair: model plus harness, versioned together, with the harness bytes snapshotted next to every result.

2 · A harness does not transfer

Measured: every cross-loaded cell scored below the tuned one. Handing 3.8 the harness built for 3.6 cost −0.9 accuracy and −1.2 precision points. Early in the campaign cross-loading looked like an +8.3-point gain at 9 books; it decayed to −1 by 23 and the claim was withdrawn.

What it changes: a new model version inherits an unproven harness, not a working one. The boring procedure — tune on the optimization split, re-measure the pair, report only held-out — is the whole finding. The 2 × 2 that supports it is two subjects on one serving stack: a real signal, not a general theory.

3 · The optimism gap is about 30 points, for both models

Measured: 3.6 95.5 % → 63.4 % (−32.1), 3.8 97.7 % → 69.1 % (−28.6) moving from the 23 books it was tuned on to 24 unseen books. This is a property of the benchmark and harness, not of either model.

What it changes: optimization-split scores are a ranking device. Quoting them as expected production performance overstates by roughly a third of the scale — which is how a model that looks excellent in a pilot turns mediocre in a portfolio. The held-out split here is spent and published with its answers, so it can never be reused as an unseen exam.

4 · Over-claiming and mislabelling are different diseases

Measured: decomposing false alarms into mislabels plus inventions: 3.8 emitted 154 claims for 123 truths with +31 pure inventions; 3.6 emitted 129 claims with +6. 3.6's errors are almost entirely mislabels — the right line, the wrong word.

What it changes: the remedy must match the disease. Over-claiming needs discipline (“do not report what is not there”); mislabelling needs vocabulary work. Confirmed experimentally: the 3.6-specific pattern-vocabulary edits helped, and attempts to make 3.6 report less made it worse. A single blended score would have hidden both the diagnosis and the failed cure.

5 · Cost changes the ranking, so it sits beside the score

Measured: the same 72-call exam cost 3.6 0.89 M tokens and 5.9 h, and 3.8 1.95 M and 21.9 h. At median per answer — 289 s versus 813 s — that is about 6 hours against about 16.

What it changes: 3.6 reaches roughly 93 % of 3.8's accuracy for about a third of the time and tokens, at better precision. Which model is “better” depends on whether a miss or a false alarm costs more where you intend to use it. Any governance pack that quotes accuracy without per-answer cost is quoting half a number.

6 · The models differ almost entirely in one scenario family

Measured: drift, recovery and volatility saturate at 100 % accuracy for every harnessed configuration. The recall gap lives in systemic (44 % vs 56 %) and noise_trap (both 50 %, worst case 0 % with 100 % FP/claim unharnessed). Unharnessed, 3.8 falsely accused the CLEAN book — nothing planted at all.

What it changes: a benchmark's discriminating power can hide in a minority of its own questions, so a single blended number tells you almost nothing about where a model will fail. Report by family; build lookalike traps; include a clean book; treat “the clean book was accused” as a headline event.

7 · Not every miss is a model failure

Measured: sc-d72b95 planted a Death drift of 0.05/yr; fitted from the data it is +0.0253 with SE 0.0251, t = 1.01 against a critical 3.18 — the minimum detectable slope at 80 % power is +0.088/yr, roughly twice what was planted. In sc-f520e5 the volatility is real (F = 17.1) and the competing “drift” reading is defensible from the same data.

What it changes: two “failures” were actually benchmark defects, and the model was at ceiling on the units the data support. Experience analysis has the same property on the other side of the table: some years you cannot tell, and the professional act is to say so. Auditing a miss before fixing a model is cheaper than the other way round.

8 · A tool offered is not a tool used

Measured: four sandboxed Python calls were offered to both models. 3.8 used them in about 24 % of held-out runs; 3.6 used them 0 of 144, after two deliberate activation attempts including mandatory-use wording. Its reasoning is sound — the evidence pack already contains the arithmetic.

What it changes: tool availability is an affordance, not a behaviour, and the asymmetry had consequences: the one hard failure in the whole study was a malformed tool request, which 3.6 structurally cannot produce. Dormant capability is sometimes a robustness advantage.

The harness did not arrive finished

Qwen3.8's harness grew v1.0 → v1.1 → v1.2 → v1.3 → v1.4 → v1.5 → v1.6e over seven optimization passes and 307 per-scenario diary entries, 268 of which involved a harness review or edit. Every pass from p3 to p7 changed both harness files; stats_pack.py grew 27,229 → 38,490 bytes in that span. The frozen bytes of v1.6e are preserved twice (stats_pack.py 16e53e0ef0b097ab, run_zero_shot.py a4b967de975625f9), and each corpus carries a harness_snapshot/ so the comparison can be checked rather than trusted.

The edits that mattered were taxonomy, not phrasing

Not “be more careful”, but: a trend that reverts at the end is still drift; a one-year spike inside a swinging line is volatility; mild overdispersion is the normal texture of these scenarios; tiny age or duration cells are unreliable however extreme their A/E looks; Poisson z-scores are invalid on an overdispersed line.

How to read a number from this project

1

Name the denominator. Accuracy is over units, FP/claim is over claims. They never add to 100 % with each other, and the 23-book and 24-book views differ by whether the recorded failure is counted.

2

Quote accuracy and false alarms together. Either can be raised by behaviour that makes the other worse.

3

Put cost beside the result it bought — tokens and wall time per answer, not just in an appendix.

4

Report held-out only. Tuning scores rank; they do not predict.

5

Cite the bytes. A number should name the corpus, the harness snapshot and the prompt that produced it.

The other study: five findings on output variance

The benchmark asks whether a model is right. The second study asks the question a regulator asks first: is it the same model twice? Repeated-invocation testing of the multi-agent underwriter — the same applicant run through the LLM path about a hundred times, Wilson intervals on every proportion. Full write-up: output variance in LLM underwriting.

1

Variance concentrates at the decision boundary, not across the book. Medical agent spread: 0 % healthy → 26 % borderline → 42 % high-risk; a separate 50-run session put the widest evidence path at a 47 % spread. Uncertainty that clusters on hard cases is predictable, which makes it routable — route by distance from the threshold, not by case type.

2

Temperature is a decision control, not a noise dial. On the high-risk profile T0.1 gave decline 58 % / loading 42 %; T0.0 gave a flat loading. Zeroing the dial changed which decision the system makes, and T0.1 — the production setting — is where the spread peaked.

3

Architecture beat the model. On the doubly-uncertain profile the debate returned loading in 100/100 runs while the Medical agent alone varied ~20 %. A most-conservative combine rule absorbed the variance — and bought conservatism with it, which is a risk-appetite decision, not a free lunch.

4

Failures are stochastic too. Unparseable output fell back to refer — safe, but the same input sometimes answered and sometimes fell back. Under parallel load the reasoning model entered a thinking loop and returned nothing; sequential calls were clean. A fallback rate and a concurrency limit belong in the decision distribution.

5

The largest variance is the part you do not control. Model developers, inference providers and the deploying company each hold a dial — which is why even a hosted T0 endpoint is rarely bit-reproducible. The variance you measured is only valid for the configuration you tested: the same rule finding 1 and finding 2 forced, arrived at from an entirely different direction.

Two of three claims died at their own noise floor

Questions were written into SPEC.md before the sweeps that answered them: 48 runs against pre-registered questions, and two of the three claims did not survive contact with their own noise floor. The same discipline the benchmark applies to models, applied to my own hypotheses.

Three repos carry claims without evidence

The experience-analysis dashboard, Standard_Inquirer and the data-schema analyser ship features and architecture, and no measured accuracy or variance study.

The argument

Four questions a convincing demo cannot answer

These are the questions an experience-study workflow has to survive before anyone signs it off. One demo answers none of them, which is why the first thing I built was an exam rather than an application.

Q1 · SUBJECTHow good is the model at the task?

How much of what is really in the data does it find, scored strictly enough that a vague answer scores nothing.

Q2 · ERRORSWhat does it miss, and what does it invent?

A miss and an invention cost different things and are fixed differently, so they cannot be averaged into one score.

Q3 · COSTWhat does one answer cost?

Tokens and wall time, printed in the same row as the result they bought — because it changes the ranking.

Q4 · PIPELINEIs the harness still fit for purpose?

What you govern is the model and the harness together. “We upgraded the model” is not an assessment.

Featured research

Can a model read an experience study?

Forty-seven synthetic portfolios — 250 000 policies each, four benefit lines, 2015–2024 — with drifts, shocks, volatility and recovery planted into the actuals by a generator that keeps the answer key sealed and leak-scanned. Two Qwen 27B models crossed with two harnesses, three runs each, scored on a held-out split with false alarms counted.

The hard scenario in the same repo plants four findings on two lines, and three of them sit inside the Poisson noise: a Death drift of +0.05/yr against ±0.045 of year-to-year variation, an IP drift of +0.03/yr against ±0.026, and one 2020 shock that is the only thing a human eye catches. Recovering a signal that is not visible is the job.

Senior data scientist and strategic analytic actuary in Sydney, thirteen years in life insurance and financial services: predictive underwriting in production at Pacific Life Re, before that Resolution Life and Zurich. Fellow of the Institute of Actuaries of Australia since 2021.

The open work sits at that intersection: actuarial judgement on one side, model governance on the other, and a stubborn insistence that neither of them survives an un-inspectable tool.

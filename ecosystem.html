Ecosystem · 13 repositories

Not a project list — a working system

Actuarial science has a long tradition of professional collaboration — experience studies, mortality tables, industry working groups. Our tools have stayed proprietary. In an era where AI is reshaping the profession, actuaries need inspectable tools more than ever, so every piece here is open, versioned and cross-referenced. Open source is not a licensing choice; it is a governance choice.

The one rule that binds the layers

After any model change, re-measure the model-and-harness pair and re-tune on recorded evidence.

Three requirements fall out of that rule, and they are the reason the layers are ordered this way rather than being a pile of apps:

1

Ground truth must be generated, not curated. A curated answer key drifts, and you cannot regenerate it when you extend the test. That is L1's job.

2

The scorer must count false alarms, not just hits. Otherwise a model can raise its score by talking more. That is L2's job, and it is the reason L2 sits above the applications in the diagram rather than beside them.

3

Every claim ships the bytes behind it. Frozen harness snapshots, pinned generator commits, decision logs, per-pass manifests — so the claim can be checked rather than trusted. That is L4–L6's job.

Why open

Underwriting rules engines are the operational backbone of insurance: they encode what risks are acceptable, on what terms, at what price. Building them is hard; explaining them to auditors, regulators and business stakeholders is harder. A closed underwriting engine is a governance black box — and the profession's answer to a black box should not be another black box.

So the design constraints repeat across layers: rules as declarative versioned documents rather than code buried in a service; escalation paths and permissions as first-class configuration rather than prompt wording; agents that propose while named humans decide; and every artifact MIT-licensed so a regulator, an auditor or another actuary can read the reasoning chain rather than take it on trust.

If you are an actuary, engineer or regulator

Corrections to the underwriting logic, the compliance mappings and the scoring rubric are the most useful contributions this ecosystem can receive. The decision record is in the repos' logs, so a disagreement can be argued against evidence rather than against a summary. Open an issue on the relevant repo.
